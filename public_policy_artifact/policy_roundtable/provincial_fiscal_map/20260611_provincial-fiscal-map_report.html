4THE TRANSFER NARRATIVE VS THE CAPACITY REALITY

The Transfer Narrative

Budget equalization is working — 47 provinces receive central transfers, formally closing the fiscal gap between rich and poor regions
63→34 consolidation will improve efficiency — fewer administrative units, streamlined approval chains, reduced overhead costs
HCM's 9% retention is necessary — national cohesion requires redistribution from high-revenue cities to lagging provinces

The Capacity Reality

Transfers arrive but don't execute — capex disbursement is declining (65%→60%), worst in the most transfer-dependent provinces. Money without capacity is delayed non-delivery.
Consolidation without capacity seeding rearranges geography — larger administrative units with the same institutional deficits, not fewer high-capacity provinces
FDI is the tacit knowledge transfer mechanism — provinces with FDI build contracting, compliance, and execution muscle. Transfers without FDI compound the dependency.
Vietnam's fiscal transfers redistribute money efficiently — but redistribute capacity not at all. The binding constraint is not the budget; it is tacit institutional knowledge.

6OPERATOR INSIGHT

The Disbursement Pipeline Is the Real Problem: The procurement-to-payment cycle at sub-provincial level has no digital backbone. Contractors face 90–180 day payment cycles. Bid evaluation is manual. Audit trails are paper-based. Every element is a solvable workflow problem. The 35% stranded capex is not waste — it is stranded working capital, and stranded working capital is a financing product waiting to be built. The entry point is not the ministry; it is the contractor receivable.

# Policy Roundtable Brief
## Vietnam Doubles Asia Pacific Investment Intent — But Durability Requires More Than a Rankings Surge

**Date:** June 12, 2026
**Region:** Southeast Asia — Vietnam
**Source:** PwC 29th Annual Global CEO Survey (Asia Pacific)
**Slug:** `20260612_vietnam-investment-outlook`

---

## Part 1: Problem Framing

### Chair's Raw Frame
Vietnam jumped from 8% to 15% investment intent share among Asia Pacific CEOs in one year — but this happened while 60% of those same CEOs froze all new international investments, meaning Vietnam's gain is a share shift within a contracting active-capital pool, not a signal of expanding demand.

---

### For Policy Experts
**Problem statement:** Vietnam's FDI surge rankings reflect CEO sentiment, not disbursed capital — and the gap between intent and actual investment arrival is a governance and infrastructure gap that policy has not yet addressed at the speed FDI entry requires.

**Hook:** Vietnam is Asia Pacific's #2 investment destination on paper. But registered FDI converts to disbursed capital at 60–70% — and no one is pricing that gap in the headline.

**Stakes:** If the infrastructure layer (financial, regulatory, logistics) cannot absorb the incoming wave, Vietnam risks becoming a preferred destination in surveys that never becomes a productive manufacturing base — validating the "Southeast Asia trap" narrative and losing the China+1 window to Mexico and India.

---

### For Builders
**Problem statement:** Every manufacturer landing in Vietnam needs treasury, payroll, compliance, and cross-border payment infrastructure in VND on day one — and the B2B product stack in Vietnam is years behind consumer fintech maturity.

**Hook:** 15% of Asia Pacific's CEO investment intent is pointed at Vietnam. That's hundreds of factory operators who need enterprise fintech that doesn't exist yet.

**Stakes:** The company that builds the B2B financial infrastructure layer for incoming FDI manufacturers captures a captive, high-ARPU client base during the entry window — before incumbents wake up and before the manufacturers lock into legacy banking relationships.

---

### For Fintech / Capital
**Problem statement:** Vietnam's rise to #2 APAC investment destination is a structural market signal for B2B financial infrastructure — but the investable thesis depends on sizing the actual disbursement-to-GDP conversion rate and the B2B product gap against comparable FDI entry markets (Thailand 2013, Indonesia 2016).

**Hook:** Vietnam is #2 on CEO surveys. The revenue pool isn't in consumer fintech — it's in the treasury, FX, payroll, and supply chain finance stack that incoming manufacturers will spend on for the next 10 years.

**Stakes:** First-mover B2B fintech platforms building for the FDI entry cohort (2025–2028) will lock in multi-year enterprise relationships before the window closes. Late movers will find the market structure already set.

## Part 3: Report Storyline Skeleton

### Narrative Spine (SCQA)
- **Situation:** Asia Pacific CEOs are in capital preservation mode — 60% froze new international investments in 2026, the highest freeze rate in a decade.
- **Complication:** Within that contraction, Vietnam nearly doubled its share of CEO investment intent, rising from 8% to 15% and becoming the #2 most preferred destination globally — a +7pp gain no other APAC market matched.
- **Question:** Is Vietnam's surge a durable structural shift or a sentiment spike that will reverse when global capital conditions ease — and what determines which outcome plays out?
- **Answer:** Vietnam has captured resilience capital, not growth capital. Durability requires the B2B infrastructure layer — financial, regulatory, logistics — to convert intent into disbursed investment and then into productive economic density. The gap between Stage 1 (CEO intent) and Stage 5 (economic density) is the real story.

---

### Section Flow

1. **Investment Intent Signals — Asia Pacific 2026** — Establish the headline data: Vietnam #2, 15% share, +88% YoY — key KPI: +7pp gain, largest in APAC cohort
2. **APAC Destination Rankings: 2025 vs 2026** — Visualize the shift across all top markets; show Vietnam's gain against a frozen backdrop — key data: US/VN gained, China/UK/SG flat
3. **The Capital Concentration Mechanism** — Explain why Vietnam wins when 40% concentrate into fewer markets: China+1 + ASEAN+ + government policy — key insight: resilience capital concentrates, growth capital diversifies
4. **Consensus vs Operator Reality** — Contrast the bullish narrative (structural, decade-long) vs the operator read (shrinking active pool, intent ≠ disbursement, corridor concentration) — key tension: 15% headline overstates actual capital arrival
5. **The Durability Ladder** — Frame 5 stages from CEO intent to economic density; position Vietnam at Stage 1 confirmed, Stage 2 tracking, Stages 3–5 unproven — key risk: disbursement conversion gap
6. **Operator Insight** — The B2B infrastructure gap: every entering manufacturer needs treasury, payroll, compliance, FX on day one — key signal: incoming FDI wave is a B2B market signal, not consumer demand
7. **Opportunity Map** — 8 enabling infrastructure layers: Industrial Fintech, Compliance Tech, Industrial Logistics, Cross-border Payments, Workforce Platform, Supply Chain Finance, Investment Intelligence, Policy Bridge
